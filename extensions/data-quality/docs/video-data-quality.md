# Reviewing video data quality

Open **Data Quality** from Cove's navigation: it lists your reviews with the number of items each one matches, sortable by name or by that number (your browser remembers the choice), and opens one when you choose its name; the browser's **Back** returns to the list. **New review** creates one, a review's row menu edits, duplicates (a copy opens at once in its editor drawer, ready to rename), exports or deletes it, and **Import** and **Export all** move reviews between accounts or keep a copy; an open review's **More** menu offers the same for that review. Use **Edit review** to configure reusable scene criteria and tag actions: it opens a drawer beside the queue, and the header's filters and scope preview the draft's criteria until you save or cancel (**Esc** or closing the drawer asks before discarding unsaved changes). New reviews start at the end of the sorted queue; existing reviews keep their chosen direction.

The filter toolbar shows the effective scene query. Its **Filters** dialog includes Cove's **Custom Fields** section for video custom fields, so a queue can require or exclude videos by a custom field such as the confirmed-absent tags that **Mark absent** records; while the review is being edited, the same dialog sets the criteria the review saves. Applying the dialog normalizes custom field criteria to their field definitions, so a review saved with incomplete criteria shows the save and reset controls after its first apply. Occurrence reviews separately offer, under **Scope** in the header, **All performers**, **Specific performers**, or **Matching criteria** (with **Edit criteria**); Specific performers with none picked, or empty criteria, reviews everyone. They also offer conditions on tags attached to that performer's appearance. A scene filter describes which videos match; performer scope describes which partners within them are reviewed. Conditions on occurrence tags apply to the same performer link, independently of video tags and performer profile tags.

Copy the URL to preserve the current search, criteria, performer scope, sort, page size, page, and direction. Reloading that URL restores its query even if the saved review later changes. Changing queue criteria brings two lightly tinted buttons into the header, just before **Batch…** (in occurrence reviews) and **More**: **Save to review** stores the effective queue criteria directly, and **Reset** restores the saved criteria. Their tooltips say that the queue differs from the saved review; where the header is short of room they show only their icons. The card grid and tag reviews offer the same, and switching between **Single** and **Grid** keeps offering them. **Edit review** changes the rest of the review, such as its name, actions and direction, and saves the queue's criteria with them; criteria you changed before opening it count as its unsaved changes, so **Cancel** leaves them unsaved and **Save review** keeps them. Queue tag bins are never saved.

Inspect the player and current tags, then choose an action on the pad under the player. The current tags look and sort as on Cove's video page: its tag badges, coloured by the tag or its group, grouped tags first. Point at an action, or focus it, to see what it would change: the line above the pad says it in words, and the current tags show additions with "+" and a dashed outline, removals with "−" and struck through, and new absences marked "absent". Actions apply and advance by default. Hold Shift while clicking or pressing the action's key to stay, as the hint above the pad says. On a phone-sized screen the pad is a grid of large buttons in key order, without keys, and a **Stay on this item** switch above them makes a tapped action apply and stay while it is on. After an action that moves on, the next video starts playing, even if you had paused the current one; the next performer in the same video keeps the player as it is. Multiple matching scene partners are processed before switching videos, and selecting another partner preserves playback.

Each action has a letter key among **q w e r t y u i o p å**, **a s d f g h j k l ö ä** and **z x c v b**: the key you pin it to in **Edit review → Actions** (choose the action's key to open the key picker; the action that held the chosen key takes this one's pinned key, or turns Auto), or, on **Auto**, the next free key in that order; **No key** leaves it to Find action. Press the key to apply the action and move on, or, in the single-item review, **Shift** and the key to apply it and stay. Press **-** for **Find action** to reach any action by name, including those without a key: type part of the name, choose with the arrow keys, press **Enter** to apply and move on or, in the single-item review, **Shift+Enter** to apply and stay, and **Esc** to close. In the multiple-video grid, **Ctrl+A** (**⌘A** on a Mac) selects every card on the page or clears the selection. The pad places each action where its key sits on the keyboard, with empty keys between, leaves out rows without actions, and dims empty keys; the grid's action bar gives each keyboard row a line of its own. The keys are fixed and work under every Cove keyboard preset; they are not listed in Cove's **?** overview or under **Settings → Keyboard shortcuts** and cannot be rebound. Holding a key down applies its action once. On a review, whenever the page takes keys, **f**, **g** and **k** belong to Data Quality: they apply the action placed on them and otherwise do nothing, instead of Cove's Filters or fullscreen, go-to chords, or play/pause.

To offer every tag of a category as an action, open **Edit review → Actions** and choose **Add from parent tags…**. Pick one or more parent tags and tick the child tags to turn into actions. They are listed most used first, and children that an action already adds start unticked. When a video, or in occurrence reviews a performer, can have only one of the children, tick **Only one per video** or **Only one per performer**: each action then also removes every other tag in that parent's tree, including tags created under it later. In occurrence reviews this also tells the review that the category takes one answer, so a performer holding different answers there is marked **Mixed** (see below). The new actions are added after the existing ones, each in a group named after its parent tag; reorder, rename, regroup or delete them like any other action, and use the command again to add children created since.

When each item needs more than one answer, say two separate properties of a performer, give the actions of each question the same **Group** (open an action in **Edit review → Actions**; type a name, or pick one from the field's list, which offers **No group**, the groups the review already has and, for a name no other action has, **New group**) and tick **Stay until every group is answered**. A group is one question with one answer per item; in occurrence reviews a performer holding two different answers of a group is marked **Mixed** (see below). The single-item review then stays on an item until it has an answer in every group, counting answers it already had: the header above the pad lists the groups, with a check and the answer for each answered one and a ring for each open one, and the actions that can answer an open group are marked. The answer that completes an item moves on at once. An action without a group applies and stays while a group is open. **Shift** and **Stay on this item** stay as always, and **Skip** moves on at any time. The grid ignores groups.

Use **Edit tags** to add or remove any tags without changing configured actions. **Save** stays on the item, **Save & next** advances, and **Cancel** discards the draft. Inputs remain available if saving fails. If some changes succeeded before an error, inspect the refreshed current tags and retry to finish the intended edit.

**Skip** changes only the cursor. It does not save tags, reviewed status, or an inconclusive answer. Skipped items and items that still match remain available in their sorted pages. The header shows the matching scene range and total, without completion percentages; its **‹ page / pages ›** pager changes pages, and clicking the page number lets you type one.

## Review many videos at once

**Grid** in the header shows the queue as cards; **Single** returns to one video at a time. The grid keeps the same header, fits the window, and puts the actions in a bar floating over the bottom of the cards. Select cards with their checkmark or **Space**, or all cards on the page with **Select all** (**Ctrl+A**); **Clear** (**Esc**) empties the selection. With cards selected, an action applies to all of them; with none, to the focused card, as the bar says. On a phone-sized screen the bar shows large buttons without keys, which scroll within the bar. Move with the arrow keys and press **Enter** to preview a video: **N** and **M** step to the previous and next card, the player takes **Space**, the arrows and **,**/**.**, and **Enter** or **Esc** closes it. **Cards** | **Wall** switches to autoplaying previews, and the size control next to the page size resizes the cards. When the review has **Queue tag bins**, the chip row lists the bins found on the page, **On this page**, with their counts: press one to review only videos with that tag, and again to return. A bin narrows only this visit: saving the review never keeps it, and it stays pressed after a save.

## Work performer by performer

Occurrence reviews can rank performers by the work left for them. Under **Scope**, set **Performers to review** to the performers that matter, for example **Matching criteria** with a gender, and set the occurrence condition to the categories to fill: **Missing at least one selected tag**, with one condition tag per category, finds appearances that still miss any of them. Switch the queue sidebar to **Performers** to list those performers with the most matching videos first, and choose one to review only that performer. **Existing answers** shows what the performer already has in each category. Check the earliest and latest videos, then use **Batch…** with that performer's answers, ticking one answer per category. The batch fills only categories that are still empty, so an occurrence that already has a different answer keeps it. **New batch** discards the results and starts over; closing the dialog discards them too and recounts that performer, so the next one is on top when you switch back to **Performers**. **Show all performers** returns to the whole scope.

Flag performers whose attributes change during their career with **Performer flags** in **Edit review → Review**: add the profile tag that marks them, then under **Affects** choose the category it concerns, a tag and everything under it (the review's condition categories are suggested first). Leave **Affects** empty to flag the whole review. A flagged performer is marked in the ranking, whose tooltip names the categories, and while chosen on its chip. While a performer is chosen, a category that takes one answer where their existing answers differ (two or more different answers, for example 40 of one size and 12 of another) needs attention too, and **Existing answers** marks it **Mixed**. An answer group always takes one answer; a condition category takes one when an action adds one of its tags and removes the tree that holds the category, as **Only one per performer** makes them. A category that can hold several answers at once shows its counts without **Mixed** or warnings of its own (flags work as before), except that a mixed answer group inside it marks its row **Mixed in** the group's name (just **Mixed** when the group is named after the category, and **Mixed here and in** the other groups' names when they are mixed too; **here**, or a plain **Mixed**, can also mean answers listed under another category, which the tooltip names); when such a category really takes one answer, make it count: give its actions one **Group**, add a **Remove tags and descendants** step for the category's tag to each of them (each answer then replaces the category's other tags), or delete them and add them again from the parent tag with **Only one per performer**, which also groups them (children that an action already adds start unticked, so adding them again without deleting the old actions would give each tag a second action). Older reviews built from tag choices instead of actions have no actions or groups, so **Existing answers** shows their counts but never **Mixed**. The item column lists the categories that need attention with the reasons, the keys that change such a category carry a small flag, and **Apply to all matching occurrences** warns when the ticked answers change one, linking their earliest and latest videos in its preview: review their videos by date, or narrow a batch with a date filter, instead of applying one answer to all of them.

## Audio reviews

Choose **Audios** or **Audio performer occurrence tags** as a review's entity type to review audios. Everything above applies, with the audio list's own filter criteria, sort options, and custom fields, and with tags written to the audio or to one performer's appearance in it.

Audios have no card grid, so both audio review kinds always review one audio at a time. The **Grid** layout and the grid's settings under **Edit review → Appearance** (**Cards** | **Wall**, **Select every card when a page opens**, **Card details**, **Tags on cards** and **Queue tag bins**) are offered only for video reviews.

Because an audio shows nothing while it plays, its description sits directly under the player rather than behind a tab, in both audio review kinds. The **Description** control collapses and expands it; that choice is remembered in the browser and is not saved with the review. An audio with no description says so instead of hiding the panel.

Tag-group reviews use the same grid and action bar, with **Cards** | **List**, and keep their selection and group assignment controls. Review configuration is account-scoped where permitted, with browser recovery support. Legacy video/occurrence progress remains stored but is ignored by the single-item workspace; the card grid resumes at its saved page and card.
